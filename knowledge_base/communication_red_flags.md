# Communication Red Flags

## Personal email addresses
Recruiters from real companies write from an official company domain, such as hr@companyname.com. Offers sent from gmail.com, yahoo.com, outlook.com or similar addresses are suspicious, especially when the sender claims to represent a large or well known company.

## Email domain does not match the company
Look at the domain after the @ sign. Lookalike domains such as companyname-careers.com or slightly misspelled names are common. The email domain should match the official website of the company.

## WhatsApp and Telegram only
Scammers prefer chat apps because they are hard to trace. An interview or offer handled only over WhatsApp or Telegram, with no email thread and no video call, is a warning sign.

## No interview and instant selection
Genuine hiring includes at least one interview or assessment. Being selected instantly, without an interview or a resume screening, is a classic scam pattern.

## Urgency and pressure
Phrases such as confirm within 24 hours, limited seats, last chance or immediate joining are used to stop you from verifying. Real employers allow reasonable time to review an offer.

## Unrealistic pay
A very high stipend for a role that needs little skill, or easy work from home earnings, is a common lure. If it sounds too good to be true, verify the company before replying.

Sources: cybercrime.gov.in, student placement cell advisories.
