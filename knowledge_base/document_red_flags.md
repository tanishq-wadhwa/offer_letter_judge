# Document Red Flags in Offer Letters

## Missing company identity
A genuine offer letter carries the full company name, a registered office address, and often a CIN (Corporate Identification Number) or GST number. Letters with only a logo and a mobile number are suspicious.

## Spelling, grammar and formatting problems
Many fake letters contain spelling errors, mismatched fonts, stretched logos or inconsistent dates. Large companies have polished templates.

## Offer before application
Receiving an offer for a role you never applied for, or after a very short chat, should make you cautious. Ask how your details were obtained.

## No named, reachable HR contact
A real letter names an HR person and gives an official phone number or email. If the HR contact cannot be reached on a company landline or official email, be careful.

## Only electronic signatures or screenshots
Offers shared as a plain image, a screenshot or an editable document with no company signature or stamp are weaker than a properly issued letter on letterhead.

## Vague role and terms
Missing joining date, work location, reporting manager, duration or stipend details are signs of an unprofessional or fake offer. Real offers state clear terms.

Sources: Ministry of Corporate Affairs guidance, placement cell advisories.
