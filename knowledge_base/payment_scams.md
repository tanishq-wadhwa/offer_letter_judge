# Payment Scams in Fake Internship and Job Offers

## Registration and training fees
A genuine employer never charges a candidate to join. If an offer asks for a registration fee, training fee, onboarding fee, ID card charge or kit charge, treat it as a major red flag. Scammers usually keep the amount small, such as 500 to 5,000 rupees, so that victims pay without thinking.

## Security deposits and "refundable" promises
Scammers often say the money is a refundable security deposit. Real companies do not collect deposits from interns or new hires. The word "refundable" is used to lower your guard. Once the payment is made, the recruiter disappears or asks for more money.

## Laptop and courier charges
Some fake offers promise a company laptop and ask you to pay courier or insurance charges first. A real company ships equipment at its own cost or gives it after joining, never before you start.

## Payment methods that signal fraud
Requests to pay through personal UPI IDs, QR codes, gift cards, crypto or a personal bank account are strong warning signs. Genuine companies do not collect money from candidates at all, and never into a personal account.

## Fake cheque and overpayment
In another pattern, the scammer sends a cheque or transfer for more than the agreed amount and asks you to return the difference. The original payment later bounces and you lose your own money.

Sources: cybercrime.gov.in, consumer awareness advisories.
